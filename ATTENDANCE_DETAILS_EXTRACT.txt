1. What KairoX is, in one paragraph
KairoX runs a leather garment factory. Its job is per-piece traceability: every individual jacket carries a barcode from
the moment the breakdown sheet is uploaded, and every single thing that happens to it — cut, fused, pasted, stitched,
stored, inspected, packed — is scanned and written against that barcode. From that one spine the system derives
production progress, material consumption, worker attendance, and payroll.
What it does not do in Phase 1: costing. The Managing Director prices a BOM by hand and emails it to the client. The
system is the system of record and the tracking layer; humans still drive the commercial decisions.

8b. Attendance · attendance
A worker who is not clocked in today cannot have production logged against them. This is a hard gate, and it is the
single most common reason a scan is refused.
Two flows:
Flow Who How
SELF Monthly workers with a loginThey check themselves in from their phone
PROXYDaily-wage workers with no loginTheir supervisor marks them present
--- PAGE BREAK ---
POST /api/v1/attendance/check-in    { "lat": 12.9716, "lon": 77.5946 } 
The geofence. The check-in is measured against the factory's configured coordinates. Outside the radius (default 100 m)
→ 403 telling the worker how far out they are. Identity comes from the token, never the body, and the timestamp is set
server-side — a worker cannot check in as someone else, from home, or at a time they choose.
⚠  Configure the factory coordinates before go-live. factory_lat and factory_lon default to 0.0 — the
Atlantic Ocean off West Africa. Until a DM sets them via PATCH /api/v1/attendance/config, every genuine
check-in is measured as ~1,900 km out of bounds and refused, and because attendance gates production, the
entire floor stops. This is the number-one first-day failure.
One row per employee per day, enforced by a database constraint. Re-tapping the card is a genuine no-op: no second
row, and the original check-in time is not moved.

16. Alembic — migrations

Three things that will bite you
1. app_user.role is a native Postgres enum. Adding a value to the Python UserRole enum is not enough —
Postgres needs the value added to the type:
if op.get_bind().dialect.name == "postgresql": 
    op.execute("ALTER TYPE user_role ADD VALUE IF NOT EXISTS 'lining_manager'") 
Guarded, so it is a no-op on SQLite where there is no native enum.
2. Always run alembic heads before setting down_revision. The chain has been branched and repaired once
already. A second root produces two heads and a schema that cannot be built.

SELECT version_num FROM alembic_version;

locate revision".

that way — it is what lets the test suite run on SQLite and keeps a future Oracle port open.

20. The ten things to remember
1. Pieces are minted at breakdown upload, not at cutting. A jacket has a barcode and a drawer before it
physically exists.
2. One barcode registry, one resolve() endpoint. 404 = never existed, 410 = retired.
3. One production endpoint. The caller never sends a stage — it is inferred per piece from that piece's history.
--- PAGE BREAK ---
4. Gate 1 fails the whole request; gates 2–4 fail one piece. One bad piece never loses the good ones scanned
with it.
5. Stock decrements once per batch, only at the two cut stages, and never on rework.
6. available is derived, never stored. It cannot drift.
7. The merge gate is about completeness, not order. Lining may arrive first; a leather-only piece is complete on
leather alone.
8. A drawer's code is static; its state recycles.
9. Rates are date-effective. A mid-period change prices each day at its own rate.
10. A closed wage run is frozen forever, and you delete a barcode, never a person.

9. Day 5 — Cutting · production (part 1)
Now the interesting part.
One endpoint, two doors, no stage buttons
The entire factory floor logs through one endpoint:
POST /api/v1/production/log 
The caller sends an actor (which worker) and targets (which pieces). Both can be given as barcodes or as ids — the
barcode door and the manual door post the identical shape, and the router resolves codes to ids before the service sees
anything.
The caller never sends a stage. That is worth restating: there is no "log fusing" button. The stage is determined one of
two ways:
On a cut screen (LEATHER_CUT / LINING_CUT), the screen fixes the stage.
Otherwise, the stage is inferred per piece from that piece's own history — the next stage on its chain.
So a stitching manager scans a tray of mixed pieces and each one advances to whatever it needed next. The manager
does not have to sort them.

Gate 1 is whole-request because if the role is wrong it is wrong for every piece in the tray. Gates 2–4 are per-piece for
one reason:
One bad piece must never lose the nine good ones a manager just scanned.
A tray of 10 with one unskilled worker mismatch returns count_logged: 9 and one entry in skill_blocked
explaining exactly which piece and why. The manager fixes one thing instead of rescanning everything. The frontend
must render all four blocked lists — a UI that shows only count_logged hides the reason.
MD and DM bypass gate 1. The skill gate deliberately fails open for an unknown or un-catalogued designation, so a
worker HR has not finished setting up does not stop the line.
Rework is permissive: re-logging a stage a piece already has is accepted and recorded as rework, and consumes no
new material — the second cut of the same piece does not decrement stock again.
